06

All five were already fragile

demo · 0:52–1:06

The trace tells you who got the lot. The compounding signal tells you where it turns into someone in a hospital.

Never say “C health grade” on stage. PRD hard rule 6 forbids claiming a letter grade measures ingredient safety, and the built system deliberately does not use it. The console’s own demo string still says it — that string is a mock, not the backend.

Speaker notes

“Then the part that matters. All five of those sites already carry critical food-handling violations — eleven, ten, eight, eight, ten. Not letter grades. Critical violations: wrong holding temperature, contaminated contact surface, unapproved source. These are the sites most likely to turn a recalled lot into someone in a hospital.”

THIS IS THE INSIGHT. Everyone builds the trace. Almost nobody crosses it with the receiving site’s own handling history. Slow down here — this is where the product stops being a lookup tool.

If asked how many sites have violations city-wide: 16,656 of the 17,204 loaded establishments have at least one. The signal isn’t rare — the intersection with an active recall is.

Speaker notes

“The brief is written locally. Hold the lot. Switch sourcing — and it names a distributor with an actual clean record on file, not one the FDA has simply never heard of. Then it fires the alert to the affected managers.”

“That trace took seconds. Today it’s three days of phone calls. And every bit of it ran on a box they own, with the network on the floor.”

If someone asks whether the LLM made the decision: no. Every number comes from a deterministic Python function; the model writes one sentence around them, and the swarm asserts the two agree. Say that as a strength — it is the difference between an instrument and a slot machine.

Speaker notes

“One thing we won’t do, which is claim more than we have. The recalls are real. The violation histories are real. The buildings are real. The supplier links are modeled — and I want to be precise about why. In the real world those links are proprietary; the operator has them, we don’t. That’s not a shortcut in our demo, that’s the FSMA-204 gap itself. Give the agent the real links and the modeled label goes away.”

“And it refuses to overclaim in a way you can check. We had a bug where it recommended switching to a distributor with zero recalls — except zero meant the FDA had never heard of them. Absence of evidence, not a clean record. We fixed it to prefer a supplier with an actual record on file, and to say unverified when it can’t tell.”

DO NOT CUT THIS FOR TIME. It is the strongest thirty seconds in the pitch — it demonstrates the honesty claim instead of asserting it. If you’re over, cut slide 9 instead.
